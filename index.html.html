Work Samples

Six pieces of work showing how I approach operations problems: finding what's wrong in financial and customer data, cleaning up records, and writing down processes.

📊 1. Billing / Revenue Audits

The Problem
Invoices and signed contracts quietly drifted apart over a year. The billing system configuration looked correct, but individual invoices went out wrong.
What I Did
Audited 33 issued invoices line-by-line against contract terms, recalculating pro-rations, verifying price-change timing, and checking percentage-based charges.
What It Found
10 discrepancies ($2,430.73 net recoverable). Identified 8 uncollected revenue issues including unapplied escalation clauses and unbilled booking overages.
Worth noting: The most expensive errors are definitional (e.g. gross vs. net commission basis). Automated checks never catch that; only reading agreements against invoices does.

🔄 2. Subscription Reconciliation

The Problem
MRR reports and contract records disagreed, and nobody had cross-checked them.
What I Did
Built a formula-driven workbook matching 37 subscription records against contracts (price, frequency, currency, dates, discounts).
What It Found
19 records flagged across 8 discrepancy types, including active subscriptions with no contract and signed contracts never billed.
Worth noting: Billing without a contract is a refund risk; a signed unbilled contract is uncollected revenue.

🧹 3. CRM Cleanup (Ops Teams)

The Problem
Fragmented CRM data: duplicate companies, missing titles, inconsistent country names ("UK", "GB", "United Kingdom").
What I Did
Profiled records, corrected data, produced an import-ready file with original values preserved, and built a full audit change log.
What It Found
39 field-level edits across 15 records and 6 duplicate groups. Flagged plain-text credentials for escalation.
Worth noting: I never delete records. Duplicates are proposed for merge with client review before committing.

📖 4. Knowledge Base Restructure

The Problem
34 pages in a chaotic flat list. Multiple conflicting versions of procedures ("use this one"), causing staff to ask in chat instead of searching.
What I Did
Audited every page (keep, rewrite, retire), restructured around user questions, and rewrote core procedures with named owners and review dates.
What It Found
9 duplicates resolved, 12 pages archived with notes, and plain-text credentials escalated for urgent removal.
Worth noting: Documentation fails due to missing structural thresholds (e.g., unstated approval limits), not writing quality.

📝 5. Process Documentation

The Problem
Bug escalations depended entirely on tribal knowledge from a single team member, stalling when they were away.
What I Did
Used a 6-prompt asynchronous framework capturing bullet fragments from the expert in ~25 mins, then drafted step-by-step SOPs.
What It Produced
Numbered steps, 2 decision rules, 3 exceptions, a troubleshooting guide, and surfaced 4 unaddressed policy gaps.
Worth noting: Open questions surface undocumented policy decisions so leadership can define them.

🏗️ 6. Knowledge Base Built Scratch

The Problem
Company relied on informal knowledge with an abandoned Notion workspace.
What I Did
Built a 3-level Notion structure organized by task, completed an ownership map, and populated critical operational procedures.
What It Found
3 of 10 mapped areas had zero owners (software renewals, equipment repairs, client data requests).
Worth noting: An ownership map reveals operational gaps an organization didn't know existed.
